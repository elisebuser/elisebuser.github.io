# Pro Tips for Climbing in Bishop

## 1. Dress for the weather

Bishop is the desert after all, which means that it is always warm and always baking in the sun. Therefore, you’d be best to leave your layers at home. Especially all those lightweight down puffy jackets that don’t do anything in the first place.

## 2. Pick your partner(s) wisely

To make sure that both of you are expanding your horizons, make sure to pick a partner with very different climbing interests and styles.

Trying to work Evilution just as the sun sinks below the mountains for those optimal sending temps? Make sure your partner is stoked on Owen’s river gorge and is hell bent on running enduro laps at the great wall till the last rays of sun have left the gorge.

Or better yet, if you’re an early go-getter and psyched to hop on the proudest boulders bishop has to offer, pick a partner who is far more excited to send pastries until 1pm then have a chill afternoon session on lowball traverses.

This will force you to strengthen your communication skills and diversify your climbing experiences.

## 3. Visit the hot springs

The Bishop-mammoth area boasts a variety of natural and non-natural hot springs within a 50 mile radius.

For an eclectic soak beneath sizzling powerlines, visit the Keough hot springs run off. If you’re feeling social, squeeze in with the other 20 people checking out the ten square feet of Crowley hot springs.

Though rarely mentioned, the city of Bishop itself also sports a variety of soaking destinations. However, one must tread particularly inconspicuously to enjoy these without cost and must also employ some level of climbing skills to get past the 4 foot tall fences that for some reason seem to pop up in the area.

Whichever hot springs you choose, just remember to keep your fingertips out of the water to avoid withering of your precious callouses. This is also a great conversation starter should you be hot springing with non-climber individuals.

## 4. Drive to Bishop from Salt Lake City

This may seem like a peculiar tip, but you will find that arriving at Bishop after driving 8 hours through the middle of Nevada will exponentially increase your appreciation for the extravagant civilization and jaw-dropping landscape of Bishop.

You will also likely feel an immense amount of relief to know that the nearest auto repair shop is now closer than 300 miles away, and that you will have the means to contact said auto repair shop should this be necessary.

## 5. Make friends with better camping accommodations than you do

For instance, if you’re sleeping in your 2006 Honda CRV, find someone with a Sprinter van camping nearby.

If you play your cards right you may be able to bum some of their hot water instead of crouching on the dirt waiting for your Jetboil to heat up.

If you are particularly lucky, you might score a few minutes hanging out in the spacious van interior instead of hunkering down in the front seat of your car and eating your milk-less mac and cheese with a headlamp and plastic spoon.

If you are the Sprinter van owner, look for individuals staying in hotels or hostels. They can offer luxuries such as showers, flushing toilets, and televisions.

These people can be easily identified via odor (or lack thereof) as well as the intensity of hair grease.

**Note:** This tip is less important in the summer.

## 6. Make friends with climbing photographers

You get sick pictures for Instagram, they get sick pictures for Instagram. Everybody wins.

## 7. Bring necessary safety equipment

### 1. Bring as many pads as possible

Ideally, the landing for every boulder should be padded at least 2 pads deep. This area generally includes a 10–20 foot radius from the base of the boulder.

### 2. Bring a stick clip

Or two. Everyone knows that Owen’s River Gorge is notorious for sketchy highball boulder problem cruxes.

Or better yet, bring your pads down into the gorge. You’ll sleep easy knowing your send hasn’t been compromised (depending on who you ask), and you will be easily identifiable to anyone wandering through the gorge.

### 3. Don't forget the flood lights

Headlamps are not necessary as you will almost certainly be back at the car by sunset. However, should you be inspired to night climb, be sure to pack at least 3 flood lights.

## 8. Go to Shatts

Do this at least once every day to avoid consuming stale and therefore subpar pastries.

Even if you are camping and climbing at the Milks, the chili cheese bacon breadsticks and 6 dollar latte are worth the hour of driving and the fear-of-bottoming-out-my-car-on-Buttermilk-Road stress.

## 9. Pick your camping spot wisely

Some local favorites are:

- BLM land just past ORG
- BLM Tablelands
- The Pit
- The Fancy Pit (Pleasant Valley Campgrounds)
- The Vons/Kmart surrounding territories
